\begin{abstract}
Indoor sensing and automotive perception place different demands on millimeter-wave point clouds. Human motion measurements depend on local articulated relations, whereas automotive detection requires coverage across targets and distances. Both operate with incomplete observations and finite computational resources. This paper outlines \TaskRadar, a shared sensing front end that translates language-specified measurement goals into temporal evidence selection and task-adaptive point generation. The system maintains a common geometric interpretation while prioritizing ambiguities that affect the requested quantity. Its planned design combines a typed goal interface, motion-aware evidence memory, calibrated task-risk estimation, and budget-aware local decoding. The evaluation will focus on indoor sensing and include a bounded automotive detection extension. We will measure application error, geometric fidelity, task-switching cost, latency, and reliability across independent participants or sequences and deployment conditions. No deployment or performance result is asserted at this stage.
\end{abstract}
